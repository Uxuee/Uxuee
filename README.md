### 👩‍🔬 About me

I am a physicist and PhD student at **Nagoya University**, working on problems related to **General Relativity, black holes, geodesic motion, quasinormal modes, and computational physics**.

My current goal is to bridge my physics background with **machine learning, data analysis, scientific AI, and research software development**. I am especially interested in problems where mathematical modeling, simulation, statistical learning, and data-driven methods meet.

I also have experience as a **Research Programmer**, working with data curation, automation, symbolic computation, reproducible workflows, and scientific software tools.

### 📌 Featured Projects

#### 🧬 Differential Gene Expression Analysis  
Reproducing and extending statistical pipelines for biomedical data analysis, including **linear mixed models**, multiple-testing correction, effect-size estimation, and gene-level interpretation.

**Tools:** Python, R, pandas, statsmodels, statistical modeling, FDR correction, data visualization.

---

#### 🔍 Computer Vision for Defect Detection  
Machine learning and deep learning pipelines for visual defect classification, including classical ML, CNN transfer learning, image augmentation, patch-based inference, and object-detection-based preprocessing.

**Tools:** Python, PyTorch, TensorFlow/Keras, scikit-learn, ResNet50, YOLO/Ultralytics, NumPy, Matplotlib.

---

#### 🕳️ Black Hole Geodesics and Scientific Computing  
Numerical and symbolic work on geodesic motion, black hole observables, perturbative models, and signal-related quantities in General Relativity.

**Tools:** Python, Wolfram Mathematica, Wolfram Language, numerical simulation, differential equations, perturbation methods.

---

#### 🧰 Structured Data Automation and Research Programming  
Data curation and automation workflows for structured research data, including rule-based filtering, regular expressions, entity matching, and reproducible code workflows.

**Tools:** Wolfram Language, SPARQL, Regex, Git, structured data processing.
